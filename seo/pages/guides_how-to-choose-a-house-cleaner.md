# How to choose a house cleaner

## Rate is the least useful signal

The cheapest quote often means an underestimate of hours, which ends in a rushed job or an awkward conversation about going over. The most expensive doesn't guarantee care. Experience with your kind of home matters more than either.

## Ask about your specific home

A cleaner who has worked a lot of 1920s bungalows knows what original hardwood and hex tile need. One who does new-build condos may not. Say what your home is when you first make contact and listen to whether the answer is specific.

## The questions worth asking

How many hours do you estimate, and is that one person or two? Do you bring supplies and a vacuum? What's not included? What happens if I need to reschedule? Are you insured? None of these are rude — anyone good has answered them many times.

## Warning signs

Reluctance to give an hours estimate. No clear answer on what's excluded. Pressure to commit to a long recurring schedule before a first visit. Cash-only with no record of the arrangement. Any of these on their own can be fine; together they're a pattern.

## Common questions

**Should I be home for the first clean?**

For the first visit, yes if you can. Walking through what matters to you takes ten minutes and saves several misunderstandings.

**What if it doesn't work out?**

Say so early and directly. Most cleaners would rather adjust than lose the work, and most problems are mismatched expectations rather than bad work.
