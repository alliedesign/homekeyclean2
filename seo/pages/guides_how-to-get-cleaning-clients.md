# Getting clients as an independent cleaner

## Be findable where people look

Most people looking for a cleaner search by their city or neighbourhood, not by company name. A listing that says which areas you actually serve and what you specialise in gets found by people who are ready to book, not just browsing.

## Say what you don't do

Counterintuitive, but the clearest listings convert best. 'I don't do laundry and I don't work weekends' filters out the enquiries that would have wasted both your time, and it makes the rest of the listing more believable.

## Price by the hour and estimate honestly

Flat rates force you to pad against the worst case, which prices you out of the easy jobs. Hourly with a clear estimate wins more work and protects you when a home is worse than described.

## Specialise visibly

Deep cleans, move-outs, pet homes, offices, eco products — a cleaner who names two or three specialities gets found by people searching for exactly that, and can charge accordingly. A generalist competes only on price.

## Ask for reviews early

The first three are the hardest and matter the most. Ask directly after a job that went well; most people are happy to and simply don't think of it.

## How listing here works

You set your own rate, keep your own clients, and trade under your own name. Clients pay us nothing — you pay for the listing. We introduce you and step back; the relationship is yours.


## Common questions

**How much should I charge?**

You decide. Look at what comparable cleaners in your area list, weigh your experience and speciality, and don't set an opening rate you'll resent in six months.

**Do I need insurance?**

Not legally required for most residential work in California, but clients increasingly ask, and commercial clients usually require it. It also makes it much easier to say yes to higher-value homes.
