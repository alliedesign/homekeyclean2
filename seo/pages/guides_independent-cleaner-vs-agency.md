# Independent cleaner or agency?

## Where the money goes

An agency takes a margin — often a substantial one — from what you pay. Hiring directly means the rate you agree is what the person cleaning your home actually earns. That usually means better pay for them and a lower cost for you at the same time.

## Consistency

With an independent you get the same person, who learns your home, remembers that the guest room only needs doing fortnightly, and knows where you keep the spare vacuum bags. Agencies rotate staff, which is more resilient to sickness and holidays but starts from scratch more often.

## Cover and continuity

This is the honest trade-off. If your independent cleaner is ill or takes two weeks off, there's no bench. An agency sends someone else. If uninterrupted coverage matters more to you than continuity of person, that's a real argument for an agency and we'd rather say so.

## Insurance and liability

Agencies typically carry cover as a matter of course. Independents vary — many carry their own, some don't. Ask directly and ask to see it. Don't assume either way.

## What we do

We list independents only, and we don't employ or dispatch anyone. Cleaners pay to be listed; clients pay us nothing. That's the entire arrangement, and it's why the rates you see are set by the people doing the work.


## Common questions

**Do I need to worry about employment status?**

For occasional cleaning of a private home by someone running their own business, generally no. If someone works substantial regular hours exclusively for you and you control how and when they work, the picture can change — that's a question for an accountant, not a cleaning directory.

**Who's liable if something is damaged?**

Discuss it before the first visit and get the answer in writing, even informally. Ask whether they carry insurance and what it covers.
