# Move-out cleaning, and your deposit

## What gets inspected

In practice: oven interior, refrigerator interior, inside every cabinet and drawer, bathroom grout and scale, window tracks, skirting boards, and the floor under where furniture stood. These are the same places a deep clean targets, which is why move-out and deep cleans overlap so much.

## Empty first, then clean

Cleaning around boxes costs more and works worse. Book the clean for after the movers are gone. Leave yourself a day of slack before the landlord's inspection so anything missed can be fixed.

## Photograph everything

Before and after, timestamped, every room. Ask whether your cleaner does this as a matter of course — several do. If a deposit is disputed weeks later, photographs are the only thing that settles it.

## Know what isn't cleaning

Nail holes, scuffed paint, a cracked tile and a broken blind are repairs, not cleaning. A cleaner cannot fix them and shouldn't be blamed for them. Sort repairs separately or negotiate them with the landlord.

## California specifics

Landlords in California must provide an itemised statement of deductions, and normal wear and tear cannot be deducted. Faded paint and worn carpet from ordinary use are wear and tear. Grease in the oven is not. If you're unsure where a deduction falls, that's a question for a tenants' rights service rather than a cleaner — this is general information, not legal advice.


## Common questions

**How far ahead should I book?**

End of month is the busiest window everywhere in the coverage area. Two weeks ahead for the last few days of a month; a few days is usually fine mid-month.

**Should the landlord's cleaner do it instead?**

They can, and they'll deduct it from your deposit at whatever they choose to charge. Booking it yourself is almost always cheaper and you control the standard.
